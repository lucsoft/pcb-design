# Supply sheet, laid out by hand.
#
# The reference an automatic layout gets judged against. Two cascaded
# buck converters, each drawn the way its own datasheet draws it.
#
# Three rules set this arrangement, all of them from review findings:
#
#   1. A support capacitor stands near what it serves. C8 and C9 used to
#      hold that by distance -- 60 units of U4's VIN pin, the bypass near
#      and the bulk beyond it -- and they hold it by the wire now: they
#      hang on the stage's ground row with their supply pins on the VIN
#      rail above, so the line under them says ground and the rail over
#      them says which rail. That is the same reading carried by geometry
#      instead of by distance, and it buys the row below. A part with only
#      rail flags is still read from the line it sits on.
#   2. Each block is a contiguous rectangle with no foreign part inside
#      it, and the blocks run in flow order down the sheet: 36 V to 5 V
#      first, 5 V to 3V3 below it.
#   3. Nothing enters the zone band or the title block. Both are
#      reserved by ISO 5457 and a plotter cuts at the frame;
#      blocktitles.py refuses to write when something does.
#
# Shunt parts stand upright, supply up and ground down, so a glance says
# which end is which. U4's four right-hand pins each turn downward at
# their own x, and the catch diode stays out of those corridors.
#
# The one exception is R26, the lower leg of the 5 V divider. Lying on
# its side in R25's own row, the tap between the two is a corner instead
# of a stretch of wire with the flag parked in the middle of it, and its
# ground end comes off the same line the stage's capacitors drop to -- so
# the divider reads as one path out of the 5 V rail and into ground.
#
# The importer gives every pin a thirty-unit lead. Upright parts do not
# care -- their footprint stays a line -- but a part lying *across* a row
# is as wide as its two leads put together, and nothing can be placed
# within that. These four lie across: their leads are cut to fifteen, so
# their footprint is seventy rather than a hundred and the rows can be
# closed up around them.
#
stub   *     5

# designator  x     y    rotation

stub   L1    5

# ---- Stage 1: TPS54360, VBUS -> 5 V ----------------------------------
# U4's VBUS pin sits at symbol-local y=-5, so the anchor goes to y=125
# and every pin lands on the 10-unit lattice.
U4    330    125     0
C5    330     40     0        # bootstrap, bridges SW_BOOT to SW over the IC
C9    208    245    90        # 220nF 100V input bypass
C8     165    245    90        # 4.7uF 100V input bulk, beyond it
R24   252    245    90        # RT_SET, straight down from the pin
R25    664    130    90        # 5 V feedback divider, top
R26    614    150   180        # 5 V feedback divider, bottom, on its side
C7     410    250    90        # compensation, COMP to GND
R27    458    235     0        # compensation series, lying in the COMP run
C6     513    250    90        # ... and its capacitor, on the ground rail

# ---- The compensation network, and the ground row it shares ------------
# U4's COMP pin runs right and turns down at x=395 to where C7 hangs. From
# that same node R27 goes *along* the run rather than down it, so the
# branch it feeds -- C6 -- can come up: C6's ground pin now sits on the
# row at y=265 with C7's, R24's, C9's and C8's, and one symbol stands for
# all five. Turning R27 cost nothing anywhere else, and it took the
# sheet's last three-corner detour and its only enclosed ring with it.
#
# The drop is not at a place of its own choosing. The router splits a Z at
# the midpoint of the run's two ends, so the vertical lands half way
# between U4's pin and C7, and moving C7 is the only way to move it. It
# has to sit left of the ground lead out of U4, which comes down beside
# it, and right of what FB_5V's lead leaves clear. C7, R27 and C6 stand
# together for that reason -- the two behind it follow so the network
# still reads as one, and the row closes up by the same move.
#
# The order is the only thing that matters here and it is fixed by the
# netlist: C7 is COMP to ground, R27 and C6 are COMP to ground *through*
# each other. Drawing R27 across the run is what makes that visible --
# C7 hangs off the near node, C6 off the far one.
D2    424    130    90        # catch diode, hanging off the switch node
L1    469    110     0        # 10uH, switch node to the 5 V rail
C10    525    130    90
C11    566    130    90

# C14 sits at y=510 rather than 500 so its ground pin lands on the same
# line as C12's, at y=520. Those two are the input pair and share one
# symbol: their ground pins sit at different offsets from their anchors,
# and that difference is what lands both on 520 and both leads on 525.
#
# C13 is the output capacitor and keeps a symbol of its own. A line
# joining it to the pair would run under the whole stage, and U5's enable
# run crosses that band -- R30 hangs below it, so the wire out of EN has
# to come down through. There is no junction record in this format, so a
# crossing reads as a connection, and that one would put SY_EN on ground.
# The line stops at C12 and C13's own symbol names its node instead.

# The stage's own ground, on one line under the input pair. U5's ground and
# R29's keep symbols of their own -- U5's lead ends directly above its own
# EN stub, and R29's right end sits across R28's column from here.
wire    GND   105,525  185,525

# ---- Stage 2: SY8089A1, 5 V -> 3V3 -----------------------------------
# Turned 180 deliberately. The symbol puts its input on the right and
# its output on the left, so at rotation 0 the stage reads backwards
# against the sheet. The flip costs upside-down pin text and buys a
# power path running the same way as stage 1 and as the page.
U5      280    490   180
C12     185    500    90        # 22uF input, hard against the 5 V pin
C14    105    510    90
R28    690    540    90        # 3V3 feedback divider, top
R29    760    560     0        # 3V3 feedback divider, bottom, on its side
L2     480    480     0        # 2.2uH, LX to the 3V3 rail
C13    560    500    90
R30     470    540     0        # enable pull-up, lying in the EN run


# ---- Fan header ------------------------------------------------------
# Beside stage 1 rather than below stage 2, because the fan runs off the
# 5 V that stage 1 makes: the block sits next to the rail it draws from
# instead of a stage away from it, and it fills the upper right the
# drawing was leaving blank.
J4      700    120     0        # not fitted; see design.yaml dnf:
# J4's ground, drawn to the stage's own ground line rather than left as a
# symbol of its own up here. The line runs at y=150 and R25's feedback
# pin sits on it at x=719, so the route dips to y=195 to pass under the
# divider and comes back up onto R26's ground pin at (649, 150).
# The dip clears R25's feedback stub, which hangs to
# y=180 -- that stub is dropped later, but this runs before anything
# knows that, and a ground wire across a feedback stub is a crossing
# either way. The symbol J4 used to carry is gone with the wire: it says
# the same thing once, and netflags places no second symbol on a node a
# wire already reaches.
#
# It arrives at the *pin*, not at R26's stub end thirty units short of
# it. The ground bus already ends at that pin, so landing there is one
# junction where landing on the stub end made two -- and R26's own stub
# then has a wire at both ends and nothing to do, so it goes.
wire    GND   680,125  680,165  589,165  589,150
# U4's ground, drawn across to the catch diode's rather than carried out
# of the pin field to a symbol of its own at (470, 120). Both are the same
# node, and one of the two symbols was saying it twice -- so one of them
# goes, and it is U4's: a lead is only drawn where the pin field is too
# crowded to carry the symbol, and the wire has taken that job over. The
# route leaves the pin row above COMP_5V and comes down on the far side
# of the wire that runs from U4's COMP pin to C7.
#
# Same as J4's: it arrives at the catch diode's ground *pin* at (500, 150)
# rather than at the free end of its stub at (500, 180). The bus already
# starts at that pin, so one junction does the work of two, and the two
# runs are collinear into the bargain -- the ground line reads as one
# line from (460, 150) to where it ends at R26.
wire    GND   385,120  405,120  405,150  424,150
# U4's thermal pad is no longer left bare, and getting there took the
# build rather than this file. The importer aims a lead by the pin's side,
# which is right for the eight pins along U4's edges and wrong for the one
# on its top: a vertical pin whose lead came out sideways, thirty units
# along the package. A symbol on that lead has nowhere to sit that is not
# to the side of the pin it belongs to, and one rooted at the stub end
# draws its bars into the IC. `--pin-stub 'GND@330,90'` in build.sh
# re-aims the lead up out of the pin, and the symbol stands on it. The
# lead is *moved* and not dropped, so the node's `NET` attribute stays
# where `epro.py diff` counts it, and `bare pins` in the build is empty.

# ---- Measured, and deliberately left -----------------------------------
# Three wire ends carry no visible name. They are the stubs whose second
# copy of a name was hidden -- a divider's two resistors each label the
# node between them, and one of the two copies goes. The name is still
# on the twin a few units away. A counter that asks "does this end have
# a name?" rather than "is this end's net named here?" reports them, and
# that is the counter's question being wrong, not the sheet's.
#
# ---- The 5 V rail's pitch, and where it stops --------------------------
# D2, L1, C10, C11, R26, R25 and J4 stood 100 to 140 units apart, which
# reads as gaps rather than as one rail; they run at about 50 now, one
# pitch for the row rather than two values with a lone hundred in the
# middle, and the four parts lying across it have their leads cut to five
# so their footprints are 50 rather than 100 -- without that the row
# cannot close below about 70. They
# can: the ground leads of D2, C10 and C11 land on one line, so no part
# needs the width a ground symbol of its own used to take, and R26 lies in
# the row rather than hanging below it. The bound is
# D2's side: the ground lead out of U4 and the COMP run to C7 both come
# down between FB_5V's lead and the diode's body, and that corridor is
# what fixes the diode's x -- 20 units further left and the two verticals
# have nowhere to go, so schwire finds no clean path for COMP_5V and
# leaves the net a label. So D2 holds and the rest of the row comes to
# it -- R25 and J4 included, which is what made room for R26 on its side
# without opening a gap beyond the divider.
#
# ---- Why this is still A3 ----------------------------------------------
# The placed circuit is 178 x 138 mm -- `build.sh` prints it -- and with
# everything blocktitles.py draws round it, the cross-sheet list and the
# remarks box beside J4 included, the drawing measures 300 x 204 mm, which
# it also prints. A4's usable area inside the zone band is 247 x 170 mm,
# so it is the annotation that overruns and not the parts, and asking
# blocktitles.py for an A4 sheet is refused for what sits in the title
# block. What is outside is not the circuit: it is the title block below
# the drawing and the remarks box beside it, with the corner between them
# occupied. Moving parts cannot fix that, and there is nothing here that
# says which of the three to give up.
#
# ---- The flag lead was 60 units, and that measurement is retired ------
# A flag carried out of the pin field puts a new line end near the
# neighbouring pin's name, and three labels read as ambiguous for it.
# That was measured on the wide layout. Re-swept from 8 to 60 on the
# compact one, every length reports zero ambiguous labels, so the lead is
# 10 now -- see build.sh.
# Swept against every counter at once: 40 units leaves three, 60 leaves
# one, 80 goes back to three -- the tip has to land past the neighbour's
# name rather than beside it. 50 through 70 all read one, so the
# setting is a plateau and not a knife edge.
#
# The other direction was tried first and is worse: pushing the labels
# further out instead. --label-offset 8 gives three ambiguous and
# nothing else; 14 adds a label on a foreign wire; 20 gives five
# ambiguous. The names are already as far out as they can usefully go.
